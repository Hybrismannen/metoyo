The Gift Whisperer - THE STRATEGIC DECK







Directive: Brand and Operational Naming

METOYO is the public-facing brand, condition, and ritual space within which this system exists.

The Gift Whisperer is the internal operational engine that runs the system inside METOYO. It functions as the working logic, prompt architecture, and execution intelligence that enables memory-driven gifting and reflective gesture.

This distinction is intentional. The operational logic, processes, routines, and system architecture described in these documents remain unchanged. Only the external naming and narrative framing evolve.

“The Gift Whisperer” should be understood as a working dwarf: an internal actor that performs the work without claiming the stage. Over time, this internal actor may evolve into a dedicated AI agent or equivalent system component. This evolution does not alter the identity, purpose, or framing of METOYO.

All references to system behavior, prompts, personas, and operational mechanics continue to apply as written. METOYO names the space and condition; The Gift Whisperer runs the place.

Gifting Has Become Transactional, Timed, and Forgettable

🕒 Obligation-driven

🛒 Commercially guided
People give what platforms push — not what memories inspire.

🧠 Emotionally outsourced
AI and ecommerce tools are now simulating empathy, not cultivating it.

📉 Attention-fatigued
In a world of alerts and frictionless delivery, presence is harder than purchase.



Strategic Implication:

Gifting needs rehumanization — not automation.
The real crisis is not in “what to buy” but how to feel while giving.

Slide 7 — Spatial Memory Input



Slide Title:

Gifts Carry Geography



Core Insight:

A gift sourced from the right place can matter more than the gift itself.



UX Structure:

During prompt setup, users are invited to add:

📍 Where they are

🎯 Where the recipient is

🌐 Where the gift should come from

This allows gifts to be:

✅ Sourced locally (gesture rooted in place)
✅ Delivered symbolically (e.g. “from where you once danced”)
✅ Prepared for future journeys (e.g. “I’ll bring this from Tokyo when I visit”)



Examples:

Tea sourced from Kyoto, where they once studied

Journal crafted in the city where the giver and recipient first met

Handmade item from the recipient’s home region



Emotional Function:

Anchors gifts in story

Shows effort through spatial care

Offers deeper narrative tension: “This came from where it began.”



Visual Suggestions:

Map sketch with three points marked: Giver / Recipient / Origin

Trident output with a source note:

“Found where she once whispered you changed her.”





Slide 8 — The Trident Model



Slide Title:

One Prompt. Three Ritual Outputs.



The Trident:

Every interaction returns these three elements:

Element

Description

Example

🜁 GIFT

A symbolic object or act matched to memory and persona

“A silver pen engraved with the words she gave you.”

🜂 SOURCE

Where to get it — locally, ethically, or to create it yourself

“Available from a maker in Malmö. Or bind one by hand.”

🜃 RITUAL

How to present it so it resonates, not just arrives

“Leave it where you last said goodbye.”



UX Considerations:

All outputs are tone-matched to the persona

Presentation guidance uses the 6 Gesture Grammars

“Where to Get It” responds to geo-input or defaults to DIY



Strategic Role of Trident:

✅ Reduces cognitive friction — everything needed to act
✅ Ritualizes giving — elevates beyond transaction
✅ Makes gifting a narrative act, not a product choice

Slide 10 — Tone System & Prompt Ethics



Slide Title:

Not Just AI. A Poetic Filter of Care



Core Principle:

The prompts don’t sell. They don’t coach.
They evoke.



How Prompts Are Designed:

Each persona has 3–5 pre-curated prompts

Prompts are tone-controlled for affective precision:
grateful, reverent, playful, brave, forgiving...

No raw user text is ever directly injected into model

Each response is filtered and poetically formatted before display



Examples:

“What did they give you without knowing?”
“What haven’t you said — not because you couldn’t, but because it stayed too close?”
“What deserves a second arrival?”



Ethical Safeguards:

No prompt reuse or personalization tracking

Quarterly tone review by a team of poets, therapists, and UX editors

Future localizations must pass gesture grammar audit

System refuses to automate grief, trauma, or forgiveness without opt-in
